1 Purpose

This policy establishes consistent requirements for children's backpacks and other belongings. Its primary rule is that parents and guardians must inspect and pack their child's belongings before every drop-off. The policy also gives educators limited authority to respond to safety concerns without creating a requirement to search every bag at every arrival.

The policy protects children from hazardous or prohibited items, supports proper medication handling, reduces disputes involving cash or valuables, and respects the privacy and dignity of children and families. The Family Day Home Standards do not require a search of every backpack or guarantee the security of undeclared valuables.

2 Scope

This policy applies in every family day home operating under the Agency and to every backpack, lunch bag, diaper bag, purse, clothing bag, school bag or other container brought for a child. Educators, approved backups and assistants apply the operational requirements. Agency staff provide training, monitoring, complaint management and corrective direction.

3 Definitions

Arrival safety check: A brief, limited visual check that may be conducted when reasonably considered necessary or practical for safety, care or compliance. It is not a routine inventory or an invasive search.

Prohibited item: An item that may not be brought because it is unlawful, dangerous, developmentally inappropriate or inconsistent with Agency policy.

Restricted item: An item accepted only with advance disclosure, required authorization and secure handling, such as medication or specialized care equipment.

Valuable: Cash, gift cards, jewellery, financial or identity documents, electronic devices or other property whose loss could cause material financial, privacy or personal harm.

Unexpected item: A prohibited, restricted or valuable item discovered after the parent has left and that was not disclosed at arrival.

Core Rules & Parent Responsibilities

4 Core Policy Rules

  • Parents and guardians have the primary and continuing responsibility to inspect every bag before drop-off and ensure it contains only items required for the child's care.
  • Cash, gift cards, jewellery, financial or identity documents, expensive electronics and other valuables must not be placed in a child's belongings.
  • Medication, emergency medication, medical devices and unusual care items must be disclosed and handed directly to the educator under the applicable authorization process.
  • Educators are not required to search every bag at every arrival. They may ask questions or conduct a brief, respectful visual check when reasonably necessary or practical for safety, care or compliance.
  • A limited check is not an inventory, guarantee or acceptance of custody for the contents of a child's bag.
  • A child must not be made responsible for carrying, declaring, guarding or accounting for an adult's cash, medication, documents or valuables.
  • Checks and decisions must be applied respectfully and without discrimination or profiling.

5 Parent and Guardian Responsibilities

  • Inspect the child's belongings at home before every drop-off.
  • Pack only necessary care items and label them where appropriate.
  • Remove all cash, valuables, prohibited items and unnecessary personal property before handing the bag to the educator.
  • Disclose medication, emergency supplies, medical devices, confidential documents or any unusual item before leaving.
  • Hand medication directly to the educator and complete all required authorizations; never leave medication loose or undisclosed in a bag.
  • Remain briefly at drop-off when the educator reasonably requests the parent's presence to resolve a concern or conduct a limited check.
  • Take back an identified prohibited, unsafe or unnecessary valuable item immediately.
  • Report missing property promptly and preserve relevant messages, receipts and other supporting information.

Arrival, Prohibited & Restricted Items

6 Educator Arrival Procedure

Morning drop-off can be brief and busy. The educator's procedure is therefore risk-based and does not require a bag search at every arrival.

  • Receive the child and observe for any immediate safety or care concern.
  • The educator may ask whether the bag contains medication, emergency supplies, valuables or an unusual item. The parent's duty to disclose applies whether or not the question is asked.
  • When a concern exists or a check is otherwise reasonably considered necessary or practical, ask the parent to remain and conduct a brief visual check of the main compartments.
  • Do not rummage unnecessarily, read documents, open sealed personal containers or treat the check as an inventory.
  • Ask the parent to remove any identified prohibited, hazardous or unnecessary valuable item before leaving.
  • Receive authorized medication directly from the parent and store it under the medication policy.
  • Place the bag in the child's designated storage area. If it contains authorized restricted care items, ensure the storage arrangement meets the applicable safety requirement.
  • Document only exceptions, concerns, refusals and unexpected items. A routine arrival with no concern does not require a bag inventory.

7 Prohibited Items

The following items must not be brought unless legally permitted, required for care and specifically authorized under another Agency procedure:

  • weapons, ammunition, replicas capable of causing alarm, knives or sharp tools not required for care;
  • illegal drugs, cannabis products, tobacco, vaping products, alcohol or related paraphernalia;
  • unsecured prescription or non-prescription medication, vitamins, herbal products or supplements;
  • toxic chemicals, cleaning agents, matches, lighters, fireworks or combustible materials;
  • small objects, loose batteries, magnets, cords or other items presenting a choking, ingestion, strangulation or burn hazard;
  • foods that conflict with the program's allergy controls or notified safety restrictions; and
  • items containing violent, sexually explicit, hateful or otherwise age-inappropriate content.

8 Restricted and Authorized Items

Restricted items may be accepted only when required for the child's care and handled under the applicable authorization process. Examples include emergency medication, prescribed medication, medical devices, specialized feeding supplies, sunscreen, insect repellent and culturally or religiously significant items.

If required documentation or authorization is missing, the educator must contact the Agency and follow the medication, health, allergy or consent policy before accepting or using the item.

Valuables, Unexpected Items & Records

9 Cash, Valuables and Confidential Documents

  • Cash and valuables are not childcare supplies and must remain with the parent.
  • An educator must decline a request to hold a valuable unless the Agency has approved a documented exceptional arrangement.
  • A valuable identified while the parent is present must be returned immediately. The educator should document the return when the item is significant.
  • If immediate return is impossible, contact the Agency. Any unavoidable temporary custody must be brief, securely managed and documented, with two-adult verification where reasonably available.
  • Educators must not count substantial cash, photograph financial documents unnecessarily or disclose the presence of a valuable to unrelated persons.

The educator, day home and Agency do not accept custody of an undeclared valuable merely because it was placed in a child's belongings. To the fullest extent permitted by law, they are not responsible for loss, damage, theft, disappearance or misplacement of undeclared valuables brought contrary to this policy. Nothing in this policy excludes responsibility that cannot lawfully be excluded, including responsibility arising from proven intentional misconduct or negligence where applicable.

10 Unexpected Item Discovered After Drop-off

  1. Protect children first and remove the item from children's access without unnecessary handling.
  2. For an immediate danger, call 911 or the appropriate emergency service and notify the Agency as soon as safely possible.
  3. For medication, hazardous items or controlled substances, secure the item appropriately, contact the Agency and parent, and follow regulatory or emergency direction.
  4. For cash or another valuable, avoid counting or displaying it unless reasonably necessary to document and secure it. Use a second adult witness where reasonably available.
  5. Complete an Unexpected Item Record before the end of the day using objective facts only.
  6. Return the item directly to the parent or another authorized adult and obtain written acknowledgement.

11 Documentation

Documentation is required when a prohibited, restricted, hazardous or significant valuable item is identified; a parent refuses to remove an item or cooperate with a reasonably required safety check; temporary custody is unavoidable; or property is reported missing.

  • date, time, child and program;
  • objective description of the item and where it was found;
  • whether the parent was present and what action was requested;
  • persons who witnessed handling, storage or return;
  • notifications to the parent, Agency, emergency services, police or Childcare Connect; and
  • final disposition and follow-up direction.

Records must not contain speculation or accusations. Corrections must be made through a dated addendum; original records must not be altered or backdated.

Privacy, Non-compliance & Accountability

12 Privacy, Dignity and Limits of Inspection

  • Conduct any check discreetly and, where practicable, with the parent present and away from unrelated families.
  • Limit the check to what is reasonably necessary for safety, care and compliance.
  • Do not search a child's clothing or body, electronic device, wallet, sealed correspondence or locked container.
  • Do not photograph or copy personal information unless reasonably necessary and authorized under Agency procedure.
  • Share records only with persons who need the information for care, investigation, insurance, legal or regulatory purposes.
  • Store records securely under the Agency's privacy and records-retention requirements.

13 Parent Refusal or Repeated Non-compliance

An educator must not forcibly seize property or physically prevent a parent from leaving. If a parent refuses a reasonably required safety check or refuses to remove an identified hazardous or prohibited item, the educator must explain that care cannot safely begin, keep the child with the parent and contact the Agency immediately.

The Agency may issue written direction, require a meeting, impose a safety condition, suspend care or end the placement in accordance with the Parent Agreement and applicable law. Immediate threats must be escalated to emergency services and the Agency.

14 Missing Property Allegations

  • Treat every report seriously and neutrally; do not presume theft or accuse any person.
  • Notify the Agency immediately and preserve relevant records and available evidence.
  • Request a written statement describing the item, last confirmed possession, timing, access and supporting information.
  • Obtain separate factual statements from relevant adults; do not conduct accusatory interviews of children.
  • Encourage the owner to contact police where criminal conduct may be involved and follow Childcare Connect or Licensing direction.
  • Do not promise reimbursement, deduct money from an educator or admit liability without Agency authorization and appropriate insurer or legal advice.

15 Roles and Accountability

Parents and guardians: Inspect and pack bags, disclose restricted items, remove prohibited items, cooperate with reasonably required checks and sign the acknowledgement form.

Educators and backups: Use the risk-based procedure, protect children, manage authorized items, document exceptions and notify the Agency.

Agency staff: Train and monitor educators, maintain records, manage complaints fairly and consult Licensing, insurers or legal counsel where required.

Home visitors: Review storage, medication controls, documentation and educator understanding during monitoring visits where relevant.

16 Training, Monitoring and Review

The Agency will provide this policy and the acknowledgement form to parents and educators, review the procedure during orientation, and monitor implementation where relevant. The policy will be reviewed annually and after a related complaint, incident, legal development or regulatory direction.

This policy must be read with the Agency's medication, allergy, emergency, privacy, records management, complaint, incident reporting, supervision and conduct policies. A stricter legal or regulatory requirement prevails.

17 Compliance Basis

This is an Agency risk-control procedure. It supports the Agency's monitoring and program-plan responsibilities and the health, safety, home-safety, medical-condition and medication requirements in the Family Day Home Standards Manual for Alberta, including Agency Standards 2, 6 and 7 and Program Standards 10, 10B, 10D and 10E. It must not be represented as a provincial requirement to search every backpack.

Collection, use, disclosure and safeguarding of personal information must comply with Alberta's Personal Information Protection Act, where applicable, and the Agency's privacy policy.
